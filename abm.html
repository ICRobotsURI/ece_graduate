At a glance

FastTrack is URI College of Engineering's accelerated bachelor's-to-master's program. It is designed to let qualified students complete a bachelor's degree and the Electrical Engineering MS in approximately five years.

Eligible URI undergraduate majorsBiomedical Engineering, Computer Engineering, and Electrical Engineering.
Graduate degreeElectrical Engineering MS.
Minimum GPA3.3.
Application deadlineJanuary 1 of the senior year.
MS requirement30 approved credits.
Double-counted creditsUp to 10 approved credits may count toward both the bachelor's and master's degrees.
Advanced-standing creditsUp to 6 additional approved credits taken as an undergraduate may be transferred if they were not used toward the bachelor's degree.
MS optionsNon-thesis and thesis. The non-thesis option is the usual FastTrack path.
Plan early: undergraduate course choices affect which credits may be used toward the accelerated master's degree.

How the credits work

The Electrical Engineering MS requires 30 credits. FastTrack can reduce the number of credits remaining after the bachelor's degree through two different mechanisms.

Double-counted credits

  • Up to 10 credits may count toward both the bachelor's and master's degrees.
  • Courses must be approved for the Electrical Engineering MS Program of Study.
  • Eligible work may include 500- or 600-level courses and 400-level courses designated and approved for graduate credit.
  • Only students admitted to FastTrack may double-count credits.

Advanced-standing credits

  • Up to 6 additional credits may transfer to the MS as advanced standing.
  • These are graduate-credit-eligible courses completed at URI while the student was an undergraduate.
  • They must not have been used to satisfy the bachelor's degree.
Do not count the same credit twice: a course used as advanced standing is different from a double-counted course. The preliminary Program of Study must identify which credits belong in each category.

Example using advanced standing

Suppose a student has 10 approved double-counted credits and also completes 6 approved credits of 500-level ELE coursework before earning the bachelor's degree. The 6 credits are not used toward the bachelor's degree and are approved as advanced standing.

When completedCredits applied to the MS
Double-counted undergraduate courses10 credits
Advanced-standing ELE graduate courses6 credits
Remaining formal graduate ELE coursework after the BS10 credits
ELE 601 Graduate Seminar after the BS1 credit
ELE 598 Master's Project after the BS3 credits
Total30 credits

In this example, the student completes 14 additional credits after the bachelor's degree. The 6 advanced-standing credits are part of the 16-credit formal graduate ELE requirement; they are not added on top of it.

If fewer than 10 credits are double-counted, or fewer than 6 credits are approved as advanced standing, additional approved graduate credits are required to reach 30. Students choosing the thesis option follow a different credit distribution and should plan it with a major professor and the Graduate Program Director.

Planning timeline

  1. Sophomore or junior year: begin planning

    Tell your academic advisor that you are considering FastTrack. Identify professional electives that may be eligible for double-counting and graduate courses that fit your interests.

  2. Before January 1 of senior year: prepare the application

    Confirm the GPA requirement, prepare a preliminary MS Program of Study, and apply through the URI Graduate School admission system.

  3. Senior year: complete approved undergraduate work

    Take the approved double-counted courses. If your schedule permits, you may also take approved graduate courses for advanced standing, provided those credits are not used for the bachelor's degree.

  4. After the bachelor's degree: complete the MS

    The normal FastTrack plan completes the remaining MS work in one additional academic year, commonly with approximately three courses or equivalent academic activities each semester.

Formal time limit: the current Graduate School Manual requires completion within two years after enrollment and coding as an ABM student. Missing that limit ends the ABM status and the ability to double-count credits.

Biomedical Engineering students should plan carefully because several undergraduate and graduate courses are cross-listed. International Engineering Program students should coordinate FastTrack with the year-abroad schedule well in advance.

How to apply

  1. Review official eligibility information

    Confirm the current FastTrack requirements on the College of Engineering website. Requirements can change.

  2. Plan the 30-credit MS program

    Identify proposed double-counted courses, any advanced-standing courses, and the graduate work to be completed after the bachelor's degree.

  3. Submit the Graduate School application

    Select the Electrical Engineering MS FastTrack/ABM option and submit the required materials by January 1 of the senior year.

  4. Monitor the application

    Application-system questions should be directed to the Office of Graduate Admission. Academic planning questions should be directed to the ECE Graduate Program Director.

Prepare the preliminary Program of Study

A preliminary Program of Study is part of the ABM application. It should list all courses intended to satisfy the 30-credit MS requirement, including:

  • courses proposed for double-counting;
  • eligible advanced-standing credits not used toward the bachelor's degree;
  • graduate courses to be completed during the MS year;
  • ELE 601 Graduate Seminar; and
  • ELE 598 Master's Project for the non-thesis option.

You may list courses that have not yet been completed. Because course offerings and academic plans can change, the Program of Study is preliminary and may be revised and resubmitted. The final approved Program of Study must match the credits used for the MS degree.

Use the Graduate School's Program of Study – Master's & Doctoral form. Check e-Campus for actual course offerings rather than assuming that a course will be offered in a particular semester.
