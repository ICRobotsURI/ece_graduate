Before you apply

Review the requirements for the degree to which you are applying.

  • Read the complete MS requirements or PhD requirements.
  • Identify faculty whose research is relevant if you want a research-based degree.
  • Confirm that your academic background provides adequate preparation.
  • Check both the ECE program page and Graduate School requirements.

The program overview provides a short degree comparison. Eligible URI undergraduates should also read the FastTrack ABM guide.

Prepare your materials

The URI application is online. A complete application generally includes:

Academic record

Unofficial transcripts from every college or university attended. Official records are required after admission.

Personal statement

Explain your preparation, academic interests, professional goals, and why URI ECE fits them.

Recommendations

ECE guidance calls for three recommendation letters. Choose people who can evaluate your academic or technical readiness.

Application fee

Pay online when submitting, unless the Graduate School or program grants an approved waiver.

International requirements

Provide language proficiency and credential documentation when required by the Graduate School.

Program-specific items

Read the selected program’s application page carefully for any additional requirements.

Submit in five steps

  1. Create your account

    Start in the URI Graduate School application system and select the intended entry term.

  2. Select the exact program

    Choose Electrical Engineering MS, PhD, or the ABM designation. The available terms and questions may differ.

  3. Complete every section

    You can save your work and return later. Make sure recommenders have enough time.

  4. Review before submitting

    Check names, dates, program choice, uploaded documents, and recommendation status.

  5. Submit and monitor

    Your application is not reviewed until it is complete and submitted with the required fee or waiver.

Open the URI application

What happens after submission?

The department reviews your academic credentials and makes a recommendation. The Graduate School makes the final admission decision and communicates it through the application system or email.

Funding is a separate question. Admission does not automatically mean that an assistantship is available. Teaching assistantships are coordinated through the department; research assistantships depend on faculty projects and funding.
