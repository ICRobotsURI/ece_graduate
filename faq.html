Programs

Choosing a path

What is ABM?

ABM is URI’s accelerated bachelor’s-to-master’s pathway. Eligible URI undergraduates plan graduate-level work early so approved undergraduate and advanced-standing credits can contribute to a separate MS degree.

Read the ABM plain-language guide.

What is the difference between MS thesis and non-thesis?

The thesis option centers on supervised research, a written thesis, and a defense. The non-thesis option emphasizes coursework and concludes with the ELE 598 master’s project.

Do I need an MS before entering the PhD program?

Not necessarily. The supplied program guidance includes a doctoral pathway for students entering without an MS. Your Program of Study and required credits will reflect your prior preparation.

Planning

Courses and Program of Study

Is the Program of Study the same as registering for classes?

No. Registration enrolls a student in courses for a particular term. The Program of Study is the approved plan showing which credits will be used for the entire degree.

Can I change my Program of Study?

Yes. Submit a revision when the courses or credits used for your degree change. The final approved plan should match the academic work used to graduate.

Can 400-level courses count toward a graduate degree?

Some approved 400-level courses may count in limited circumstances, but the allowed amount, approval requirements, and minimum grade matter. Check the rules for your exact degree before enrolling.

Can I transfer graduate credits?

Potentially, within Graduate School and program limits. Transfer credit requires review and approval; it should be addressed early when building the Program of Study.

People and funding

Advising and assistantships

Who is my advisor?

For non-thesis MS students, the Graduate Program Director initially provides academic advising until a faculty member supervises the ELE 598 project. Thesis MS and PhD students need a faculty major professor for their research.

Does admission guarantee funding?

No. Teaching and research assistantships depend on departmental needs, faculty grants, fit, and available funding. Ask directly and do not assume an admission offer includes support unless the offer says so.

How do I find a research advisor?

Review faculty research areas, read recent work, and contact faculty with a concise explanation of your background and specific interests. A good match is about research alignment and working expectations—not only available money.

Progress

Enrollment and graduation

Where do I find the correct graduate form?

Use the live Graduate School forms portal. Do not rely on an old downloaded blank form.

Where are graduation and defense deadlines?

The Graduate School academic calendar publishes term-specific deadlines. Plan for department review before the final university deadline.

Can I simply stop registering if I need time away?

Do not assume so. A formal leave of absence or withdrawal may be required. Funding and international-student status can add consequences, so consult the relevant offices before changing enrollment.
