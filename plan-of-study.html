Program of Study

The Program of Study identifies the courses and credits a student intends to use to satisfy degree requirements.

Definition

The Program of Study is the approved list of coursework and research credits that will satisfy degree requirements.

Purpose: the form documents how each degree requirement will be satisfied and provides a basis for academic review and approval.

It is separate from semester registration and the academic transcript. If the courses used for the degree change, a revised Program of Study must be submitted.

Collect these before you start

  • The requirements for your exact degree and option.
  • Your current transcript or list of completed graduate courses.
  • Your expected courses for future semesters.
  • Any proposed transfer or advanced-standing credits.
  • Your advisor or major professor’s guidance.
  • The current official Program of Study form.

Build the plan step by step

  1. Write down the rules first

    List total credits, required ELE credits, seminar, research/project requirements, course-level limits, and any option-specific rules.

  2. Add completed courses

    Use the exact course prefix, number, title, credits, and term shown on your record.

  3. Add planned courses

    Choose realistic future coursework that fills the remaining requirements. Confirm that courses are likely to be offered.

  4. Add research or project credits

    For thesis, dissertation, or non-thesis project work, use the correct course number and allowed credit range.

  5. Run every credit check

    Do not stop when the total reaches 30 or 72. Confirm every category and limit independently.

  6. Review and submit

    Review with your advisor or major professor, complete the electronic form, and follow the approval workflow.

Checks before submission

Use this as a conversation checklist. Exact limits depend on the degree and current official rules.

Total credits

Does the plan reach the required total without counting disallowed credits?

Graduate ELE credits

Does it include the required amount of formal graduate electrical engineering coursework?

Course level

Are 400-level courses within the permitted maximum and approved where necessary?

Independent study

Are special-problems credits within the applicable limit?

Seminar

Are required ELE 601/602 enrollments distinguished from credits that count toward the degree?

Research or project

Does the plan use the correct ELE 598, 599, or 699 credits for the selected pathway?

Common mistake: a plan can have enough total credits and still be invalid because one category is short or another exceeds its limit.

What if the plan changes?

That is normal. If you take different courses, change research direction, or adjust transfer credits, submit a revised Program of Study. The final approved plan should match the academic work used for your degree.

Do not wait until graduation to discover that your transcript and approved plan disagree.
