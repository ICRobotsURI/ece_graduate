Accelerated Bachelor’s to Master’s

ABM program

ABM allows eligible URI undergraduates to begin planning for an MS while completing the bachelor’s degree. Approved undergraduate coursework and advanced-standing credits may contribute to the master’s program.

General sequence: plan the combined curriculum, apply while enrolled as an undergraduate, complete the bachelor’s requirements, and continue with the remaining master’s requirements.

Eligibility considerations

  • Current enrollment as an eligible URI undergraduate.
  • Required academic performance.
  • An approved plan for graduate-level coursework.
  • Sufficient time to complete the selected MS requirements.

Current application deadline

The URI Graduate School application page currently lists January 1 for Electrical Engineering MS–ABM fall admission. Older departmental materials list a different date; verify the current deadline before applying.

Application information

Research-based master’s

MS thesis

The thesis option combines graduate coursework with a focused research project supervised by a major professor and committee. You write a thesis and defend the work.

Choose it when:

  • You want meaningful research experience.
  • You are considering doctoral study.
  • You have or can identify a faculty advisor and research area.
  • You are comfortable with a less predictable research timeline.
Planning implication: do not treat the thesis as one final-semester assignment. Advisor selection, committee formation, proposal approval, research, writing, and defense happen across multiple semesters.

Coursework + project master’s

MS non-thesis

The non-thesis option emphasizes advanced coursework and concludes with ELE 598, the master’s project, instead of a thesis.

Choose it when:

  • Your priority is advanced technical coursework and professional development.
  • You prefer a bounded project over an extended thesis.
  • You do not need a thesis-centered research experience for your goals.

You will still need a coherent Plan of Study and must satisfy the department’s graduate ELE, seminar, grade, and total-credit requirements.

Doctoral research

PhD

A PhD is not simply “more classes after an MS.” Its central purpose is to train you to conduct original, independent research.

The path includes:

  • A major professor and doctoral committee.
  • An approved Program of Study.
  • The departmental qualifying examination.
  • Written and oral comprehensive examinations.
  • An approved dissertation proposal.
  • Original research, a dissertation, and an oral defense.
Ask before choosing: Do I want research to be my primary work for several years? Which faculty member and research environment fit my interests? What funding is actually available?
