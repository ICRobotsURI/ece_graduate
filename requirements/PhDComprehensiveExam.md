# Proposed ECE PhD Comprehensive Examination Policy

> **Status:** Draft for departmental review. This document is not yet official policy.
>
> **Policy hierarchy:** The University of Rhode Island Graduate School Manual controls. College of Engineering requirements apply next. The Department of Electrical, Computer and Biomedical Engineering may impose additional requirements that do not conflict with either higher-level source.

## 1. Purpose and required components

1. Every PhD student must complete the Comprehensive Examination at or near, but no later than twelve months after, completing the formal courses listed in the approved Program of Study.

2. The examination assesses the student's intellectual capacity and the adequacy of the student's training or scholarly research.

3. The Comprehensive Examination has two required parts:

   1. Part I: Written Examination.
   2. Part II: Oral Examination.

4. The written examination is completed first. The oral examination may occur only after the student has passed the full written examination.

5. The student passes the Comprehensive Examination only by passing both parts.

## 2. Committee requirements

### 2.1 Doctoral committee

The student must have a complete doctoral committee, established with Graduate School approval, before scheduling the Comprehensive Examination. All committee members must hold Graduate Faculty status. The doctoral committee consists of:

1. the major professor;
2. one faculty member from within the department; and
3. one Graduate Faculty member from outside the department.

### 2.2 Oral comprehensive examination committee

The oral comprehensive examination committee consists of the doctoral committee plus one additional Graduate Faculty member. The student nominates the additional member in consultation with the major professor. The additional member:

1. must not have served on the student's committee before the oral comprehensive examination;
2. must be from a different department in an area allied to the student's research; and
3. serves as an impartial Graduate Faculty representative who helps ensure that the examination follows applicable procedures and policies.

The major professor chairs both the written and oral comprehensive examination committees.

## 3. Scheduling

1. The student may coordinate possible dates and other logistics, but the major professor, Graduate Program Director or department chair, or Dean of the Graduate School is responsible for arranging the examination in consultation with the student and the appropriate committee members.

2. The student must submit the current Graduate School scheduling form at least two weeks before the written examination begins.

3. The scheduling form must identify:

   - the date on which the written examination will begin; and
   - the planned oral-examination date agreed upon by all prospective oral committee members.

4. The planned oral date is contingent upon the student passing the written examination and the major professor receiving permission from the Dean of the Graduate School to hold the oral examination.

5. The oral examination must normally occur within four weeks after successful completion of the written examination. The schedule should allow at least a few days between the two parts so the written examination can be evaluated.

## 4. Part I: Written Examination

### 4.1 Preparation and administration

1. The written examination must last at least eight hours. The committee may establish a longer period, up to one week, when appropriate for the selected format.

2. The major professor prepares, administers, and evaluates the examination in consultation with all members of the doctoral committee, including the member from outside the department.

3. Doctoral committee members must agree on the examination format and questions and may contribute their own questions or assignments.

4. The examination must assess the student's intellectual capacity and the adequacy of the student's preparation for scholarly research.

### 4.2 Permitted formats

The doctoral committee has substantial flexibility to select a format appropriate to the student's field and research. The written examination may include one or more of the following:

- a dissertation proposal;
- a research paper;
- a project proposal responding to an actual or representative grant opportunity;
- focused technical questions concerning the student's research; or
- broader questions concerning the student's research area.

### 4.3 Evaluation and result

1. The doctoral program reviews the written results and makes the final determination of whether the student passed.

2. Unanimous approval by all members of the doctoral committee is required to pass the written examination.

3. The major professor coordinates evaluation of the written work, but all doctoral committee members must have an opportunity to participate.

4. The student and the Dean of the Graduate School must be informed promptly of the result using the Graduate School's current reporting procedure and form.

5. If the student fails the written examination, the oral examination does not take place.

## 5. Part II: Oral Examination

### 5.1 Format

1. The oral examination may last no more than two hours, excluding committee deliberations.

2. When the oral examination is scheduled, the major professor must provide the student and committee members with a written description of the expected format.

3. The customary ECE format is for the student to present the research and defend the dissertation proposal, followed by questions from the committee. The committee may prescribe a different format consistent with the purpose of the examination.

4. Committee members may ask questions, provide feedback, and suggest improvements to the research or proposal.

5. At the end of the examination, the student leaves the meeting while the committee deliberates and votes.

### 5.2 Attendance and location

1. The full oral examination committee must be present for the entire examination, including deliberation and the final vote.

2. Physical attendance by the student and all committee members is customary. A physical, virtual, or hybrid format may be used when agreed upon unanimously during scheduling and permitted by current Graduate School rules.

3. At the discretion of the major professor and examination chair, other faculty members may attend as non-voting observers. An observer may ask questions only when recognized by the major professor and may be asked to leave at any time.

4. A non-faculty observer may attend only with the unanimous consent of the student and doctoral committee.

### 5.3 Evaluation and result

1. Unanimous approval by every member of the oral examination committee is required to pass.

2. The committee informs the student orally of the result as soon as deliberations are complete.

3. The examination chair promptly reports the result to the Dean of the Graduate School using the current Graduate School form. The form must be digitally signed by all oral committee members and acknowledged by the departmental official required by the current Graduate School form and procedure.

## 6. Failure and reexamination

### 6.1 Written examination

1. A student who fails the written examination may be permitted one reexamination in the failed part or parts.

2. Reexamination is not automatic. It must be recommended by the doctoral committee and approved by the Dean of the Graduate School.

3. An approved written reexamination may occur only after at least two weeks have passed and before one year has elapsed.

### 6.2 Oral examination

1. A student who fails the oral examination may be permitted one oral reexamination.

2. Reexamination is not automatic. It must be recommended by the oral examination committee and approved by the Dean of the Graduate School.

3. An approved oral reexamination may occur only after at least two weeks have passed and before one year has elapsed.

### 6.3 Departmental dismissal rule

1. If a committee does not recommend reexamination, or the Dean of the Graduate School does not approve it, the Department will recommend dismissal from the PhD program.

2. A student who fails an authorized reexamination is not eligible for another examination. The Department will recommend dismissal from the PhD program, subject to applicable Graduate School procedures.

## 7. Period of validity

Passing written and oral Comprehensive Examination results remain valid for five years from the date the examinations are taken.

## 8. Authority

This proposed policy is based on the hierarchy below:

1. URI Graduate School Manual sections 7.57.1 through 7.57.1.3, 8.34.5 through 8.34.6, and 8.43.2 through 8.43.3.
2. College of Engineering graduate examination requirements.
3. Additional ECE committee, scheduling, format, and progress requirements stated in this document.
