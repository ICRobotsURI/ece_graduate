# Electrical Engineering Ph. D. Degree Program Requirements

1.  Minimum of 72 credits in engineering, mathematics, and/or science
    courses.

    1.  For requirements governing the first 30 credits (and for
        the M. S. degree) see the companion document “Electrical
        Engineering M. S. Degree Program Requirements.” The M. S. degree
        may count for up to 30 of the 72 credits required for the Ph. D.

    2.  The remaining 42 credits must be completed at URI. \[section
        7.54\].

        1.  Minimum of 18 credits, beyond the first 30 credits, in
            formal graduate engineering, mathematics, and/or science
            courses. Up to three credits of special problems (ELE 591
            and/or ELE 691), sometimes called “independent study,” may
            be counted toward this requirement. Maximum of nine credits
            of special problems (ELE 591 and/or ELE 691) and special
            topics (ELE 594 and/or ELE 694) may be counted toward this
            requirement. Department seminar (ELE 601 and ELE 602) may
            not be counted toward this requirement.

        2.  At least 18, but no more than 24, credits of dissertation
            (ELE 699). Additional ELE 699 credits may be completed for
            no program credit.

    3.  All full-time graduate students (M. S. or Ph. D.) are required
        to enroll in ELE 601 every semester the course offered.

# Notes:

1.  The ELE Graduate Affairs Committee must approve all courses outside
    of engineering, math, physics, and statistics to be counted toward
    the credit requirements.

2.  All coursework beyond the first 30 credits (or the M. S. degree)
    that will be counted toward the doctorate requirements must be at
    the 500- or 600-level \[section 9.20\]. No 400-level credits may be
    counted toward the 42 credits required beyond the first 30 credits
    (or the M. S. degree) \[section 9.21\].

3.  To fulfill credit requirements:

    1.  a course grade of B- or higher is required in any 400-level
        course;

    2.  a course grade of C or higher is required in any 500- and
        600-level courses.

4.  All But Dissertation (ABD) Status: Students who have completed all
    their course requirement, have passed PhD qualifying and
    comprehensive exams and have an approved dissertation proposal are
    considered ABD students.

5.  An ABD student may register for reduced 3-credit ELE 699 to be
    considered full-time. Up to 4 semesters of ABD status is possible.

6.  ABD students may register for reduced 1-credit for up to 2 semesters
    within their graduation.

1.  Program of Study (PoS) \[section 7.52\]

    1.  specifies major professor (“dissertation advisor”)

    2.  full-time: submit no later than end of third semester

    3.  part-time: submit no later than end of fourth semester

    4.  can be revised, but must match courses on transcript

    5.  Grad School form “Program of Study – PhD”

2.  Establish Doctoral Committee \[section 7.53 and 8.43.2\]

    1.  specifies doctoral committee (major prof, inside dept faculty,
        outside dept faculty)

    2.  submitted with PoS

    3.  Grad School form “Establishment of a Doctoral Committee”

3.  Students must be registered for any semester or summer term in which
    they take a Qualifying Exam or Comprehensive Exam or defend a
    dissertation. \[section 7.61\]

4.  Qualifying Exam (QE) \[section 7.55\]

    1.  ELE Department requires QE of *all* Ph. D. students (regardless
        of M. S. status)

    2.  full-time students who start the program in the Fall must take
        QE during the third semester; students who the start the program
        in the Spring must take QE during the fourth semester (ELE).

    3.  part-time students must take QE during the fifth semester (ELE)

    4.  QE is a four-hour written exam, closed-book, closed-notes,
        offered during Fall semester (ELE)

    5.  format: student selects three regular graduate level ELE courses
        (500 and above, excluding ELE 591) to be tested on, and notifies
        the Graduate Program Director of the selection by the required
        deadline. Two questions from each course selected will appear on
        the exam, a total of six questions; the student is required to
        answer four of the six questions. (ELE)

    6.  Grad School form “Results of a Qualifying Examination”
        (submitted by ELE dept)

    7.  non-pass students may retake QE once, the following year

5.  Comprehensive Exam (CE) two parts: Written and Oral \[section
    7.57.1\]. The student must pass both parts to pass the Comprehensive
    Exam.

    1.  no later than 12 months after completing “formal courses” listed
        on PoS

    2.  at least 12 months before dissertation defense (ELE)

    3.  CE is scheduled separately for each student by major professor

    4.  Grad School form “Request to Schedule the Written and Oral
        Doctoral Comprehensive Examination”

    5.  Part One – Written \[section 7.57.1.1\]. ELE: Research Plan

        1.  coordinated by major professor, adjudicated by doctoral
            committee

        2.  at least eight hours long

        3.  format: student develops a research plan, typically 50–100
            pages, providing a full prob- lem statement, a literature
            review, a full bibliography, a technical description of pre-
            liminary results, and a clear statement of the work to be
            done before the dissertation defense (ELE)

        4.  requires unanimous committee decision for “passing” result

        5.  rad School form “Results of Written Doctoral Comprehensive
            Examination”

    6.  Part Two – Oral; \[section 7.57.1.2\] ELE: Research Plan Defense

        1.  coordinated by major professor, attended by dissertation
            committee

        2.  will be completed within four weeks of Written part (ELE)

        3.  doctoral committee + outside member = oral CE committee
            (major professor is chair)

        4.  format: a defense of the Research Plan (from the Written
            part) and all general knowledge contained within the Plan
            (ELE)

        5.  typically two hours long

        6.  non-faculty may not attend

        7.  assessment: (ELE)

            1.  Is the research problem well-defined and significant?

            2.  Does the student have a solid approach to solve the
                problem?

            3.  Are there sufficient preliminary results to support the
                Research Plan?

            4.  Will the final product be sufficient for a Ph. D.?

            5.  For the Oral part, does the student have sufficient
                communication and presentation skills to present and
                defend the work?

        8.  requires unanimous committee decision for “passing” result

        9.  if decision is “fail”: must re-take exam within 12 weeks
            (ELE)

        10. after “passing” result, student writes Dissertation Proposal
            (ELE)

        11. Grad School form “Results of Oral Doctoral Comprehensive
            Examination” (Grad School sends blank form to major
            professor after Written is passed)

    7.  Comprehensive Exam result valid for five years \[section
        7.57.1.3\]
